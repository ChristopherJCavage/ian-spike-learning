## Which Kit You Need for Which Build

We build with three kits: LEGO® Education SPIKE™, the LEGO® Education
Simple Machines set, and Elenco® Snap Circuits®. Every build says up front
which one it needs. The badge on its card shows the boxes, and the
checklist under What You Need spells out the set numbers.

## LEGO® SPIKE™

LEGO® SPIKE™ comes in three boxes: Essential, Prime, and the Prime Expansion.
At the Contemporary Chinese School of Arizona (CCSA), the LEGO® robotics
club sticks to SPIKE™ Essential. An hour on Saturday is about one box, and
that's the one the club has. At home we mix and match. We have all three,
and the pieces are sorted into tackle boxes.

Picture: our own LeoCAD render of the Small Hub with the color sensor and a
motor plugged in. No official product photos anywhere on this page.

### SPIKE™ Essential, Set 45345

The club's box, made for kids around six to ten. It has a
Small Hub with two ports, two small motors, a color sensor, a 3x3 light
matrix, four LEGO® minifigures, and a tray of bricks. Two ports means every model
picks exactly two of motor, motor, sensor, or matrix.

[Official Product Page](https://education.lego.com/en-us/products/lego-education-spike-essential-set/45345/)

### SPIKE™ Prime, Set 45678

The bigger sibling for older kids: a Large Hub with six ports, bigger
motors, and distance and force sensors. We have one at home. None of the
Club-Ready builds need it.

[Official Product Page](https://education.lego.com/en-us/products/lego-education-spike-prime-set/45678/)

### SPIKE™ Prime Expansion, Set 45681

An add-on for Prime with a lot more LEGO® Technic® pieces, one more motor, and a
second color sensor. It has no hub of its own, so it only works alongside
a Prime set.

[Official Product Page](https://education.lego.com/en-us/products/lego-education-spike-prime-expansion-set/45681/)

### About the Retirement

Anchor: `#spike`

LEGO® Education retired the SPIKE™ line, and company sales of Essential
and Prime ended on June 30, 2026. Nothing about the kits stopped working.
The SPIKE™ App gets bug fixes and support for new operating systems until
June 30, 2031, and the official teacher resources stay online until then
too. Resellers still have some stock, and there's a healthy secondhand
market.

After 2031 the app will still be downloadable but won't be updated, so a
future iPad or Chromebook might not run it. If that day comes, there's an
open-source firmware called [Pybricks](https://pybricks.com/) that runs on
SPIKE™ hubs and doesn't depend on the LEGO Group's timeline. I haven't
tried it myself yet, so treat it as a lead and not a recommendation.

Source for the dates: [LEGO® Education, SPIKE™ portfolio retirement](https://education.lego.com/en-us/spike-update-2026/).

## LEGO® Simple Machines

### Simple Machines, Set 9689

The LEGO® Education Simple Machines set. It came out in 2011 and has 204
pieces: gears, levers, pulleys, and wheels, with no motor, no hub, and no
app, so it doesn't go out of date the way a programmable kit can. The LEGO
Group no longer sells it. It turns up secondhand. I don't have a retirement
date from the LEGO Group, so this page doesn't guess one.

## Elenco® Snap Circuits®

Elenco® Snap Circuits® comes in boxes that share one snap system, and we
have two of them. The parts snap onto a plastic grid, so we snap together
our own circuits with no solder and no code. It isn't a LEGO® product.
The blocks from the two boxes can share a grid. The limit is the battery.
Junior parts are made for 3 volts, from two AA batteries. The Beginner
holder uses three AA batteries, which is 4.5 volts. The Junior manual
warns that the extra voltage can burn out a part, including the lamp
wire, so a Junior part stays off that holder.

### Snap Circuits® Beginner, SCB-20

The small box: 14 parts on a mini grid, run by three AA batteries. A lamp,
a red-and-yellow LED, a motor that glows while it spins, a horn, two
switches, and the wires to join them. It doesn't upgrade to the bigger
sets, but its blocks snap into them.

[Official Product Page](https://shop.elenco.com/consumers/snap-circuits-beginner.html)

### Snap Circuits® Junior, SC-100

The bigger box: about 30 parts on a full-size grid, run by two AA
batteries, with 100 experiments in its manual. It adds a speaker, a light
sensor, and sound modules, and it's where the upgrade kits to the larger
Snap Circuits® sets start.

[Official Product Page](https://shop.elenco.com/consumers/snap-circuits-jr-100-experiments.html)

`sc100`. Every kit we own has a card in its family's section, and a new
family gets its own section with a line above it. The page never counts or
names builds, so it doesn't go stale. The footer's Elenco® trademark line
